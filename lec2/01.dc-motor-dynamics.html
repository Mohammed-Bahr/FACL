1. The Ideal DC Motor

A DC motor converts electrical energy into mechanical rotation. The physics gives two fundamental equations:

Torque Equation:
𝜏=𝐾𝑡⋅𝑖

Back-EMF Equation:
𝐸=𝐾𝑡⋅𝜔

What is Back-EMF in a DC Motor ?

Back-EMF𝐸 is the voltage generated by the motor's rotating coil as it moves through a magnetic field. It opposes the applied supply voltage 𝑉.

Key Idea

Higher speed→Higher back-EMF→Lower current

Back-EMF is proportional to motor speed:

𝐸=𝐾𝑒𝜔

where:

  • 𝐸 = back-EMF (V)
  • 𝐾𝑒 = motor constant
  • 𝜔 = angular speed (rad/s)

Motor Electrical Equation

Using KVL:

𝑉=𝑅𝑖+𝐿𝑑𝑖𝑑𝑡+𝐸

where:

  • 𝑉 = supply voltage
  • 𝑅 = coil resistance
  • 𝐿 = coil inductance
  • 𝑖 = armature current
  • 𝐸 = back-EMF

What Happens During Operation?

1. Startup:

𝜔=0⇒𝐸=0

There is no back-EMF, so the starting current is high:

𝑖≈𝑉𝑅

2. Motor speeds up:

𝜔↑⇒𝐸↑

The increasing back-EMF opposes the supply voltage and reduces the current.

3. High / no-load speed:

𝐸≈𝑉

The current becomes very small, so the motor produces very little torque.

Remember

Back-EMF is the motor's own generated voltage that increases with speed and limits the current.

The motor is called ideal because we assume all electrical energy converts to mechanical energy (no losses).
In reality there's friction, poor assembly, air resistance, and heat.

Remember what is 𝐾𝑡 ?

𝐾𝑡 is the motor's construction: the magnetic field strength 𝐵 , the coil cross-section area 𝐴, and the number of turns 𝑁.
Roughly, 𝐾𝑡 ∝𝑁𝐵𝐴, so a stronger magnet or more turns gives more torque per amp.

Practical insight: a high 𝐾𝑡 means more torque per ampere (fewer amps needed for a given torque).
But as you'll see, it also means a lower top speed that's a fundamental trade-off in motor design.

Why 𝐾𝑡 =𝐾𝑒: for an ideal motor, electrical power in = mechanical power out:
𝑒⋅𝑖=𝜏⋅𝜔⇒𝐾𝑒𝜔𝑖=𝐾𝑡𝑖𝜔⇒𝐾𝑒=𝐾𝑡Many books call this single value the motor constant 𝐾𝑚. We'll use 𝐾𝑡 for both.

    Voltage⟷Torque⟷Angular Speed

6. The physical intuition: two time scales

The document's "light object rings" analogy is vague. A sharper way to see it is to define two time constants:

  • 𝜏𝑚 ≲4𝜏𝑒 (light rotor): the electrical and mechanical dynamics are comparable. They interact like an LC pair exchanging energy, which gives complex poles and ringing.

9. Where This Is Heading Gearboxes and Loads

In robotics, motors are almost never used alone; a gearbox sits between the motor and the arm/wheel. Its purpose:

Gearbox: ↓ maximum speed, ↑ torque

It trades the motor's excessive speed (which we saw is cheap to get) for torque (which is expensive). Gears also let you convert rotation into linear motion (belt/pulley).

Part 1: The Denavit Hartenberg (DH) Convention

Why Do We Need the DH Convention?

  • Problem: For an open kinematic serial manipulator composed of multiple rigid links connected by motorized joints, each link can rotate or translate in 3D space.
    • If we choose coordinate frames arbitrarily for each link, constructing the transformation from the base to the end-effector requires 6 parameters per link (3 translations + 3 Euler angles), leading to complex, non-standardized equations that must be recalculated from scratch for every robot design.
  • Fix: The Denavit–Hartenberg (DH) convention solves this by imposing geometric constraints on frame placement:
    1. Every coordinate frame transformation between adjacent links is uniquely described by only 4 parameters instead of 6.
    2. The entire kinematic modeling process becomes systematic and programmable: software libraries (e.g., in ROS or MATLAB) can automatically construct forward kinematics and Jacobians directly from a simple numerical table.

Coordinate Frame Assignment Rules

Tooooooo important:

Correct frame assignment is the single most critical step in robot kinematics any error in axis placement corrupts all subsequent transformation matrices.

Follow these standardized rules:

  1. Base Frame {0}:

    • Placed at the base of the robot (Joint 1).
    • 𝑧0 is aligned with the motion axis of Joint 1.
    • Its origin 𝑂0 and orientation (𝑥0,𝑦0,𝑧0) remain permanently fixed.

  2. Frame Ownership & Joint Allocation:

    • Crucial Rule: Coordinate frame {𝑖 −1} is attached to Joint 𝑖, but physically belongs to Link 𝑖 −1.
    • Axis 𝑧𝑖−1 is the axis of actuation for Joint 𝑖.
    • Frame {𝑖} is located at the distal end of Link 𝑖 (at Joint 𝑖 +1).

  3. 𝑍-Axis Assignment (Actuation Axis محور التشغيل):

    • Revolute joint: 𝑧𝑖−1 is the axis of rotation.
    • Prismatic joint: 𝑧𝑖−1 is the axis of linear translation.

  4. 𝑋-Axis Assignment (The Common Normal):

    • 𝑥𝑖 is chosen along the common normal perpendicular to both 𝑧𝑖−1 and 𝑧𝑖, pointing from Joint 𝑖 toward Joint 𝑖 +1.

  5. 𝑌-Axis Assignment (Right-Hand Rule):

    • Once 𝑥𝑖 and 𝑧𝑖 are assigned, 𝑦𝑖 is determined automatically to form a right-handed orthogonal system: 𝑦𝑖=𝑧𝑖×𝑥𝑖⟺𝑥𝑖×𝑦𝑖=𝑧𝑖

  6. End-Effector Frame {𝑛}:

    • Attached to the tool tip or gripper center (𝑂𝑒). The origin is placed at the working point, with 𝑧𝑛 pointing in the approach direction and 𝑥𝑛 aligned with the tool opening.
2D / Planar Robot Drawing Rule

In planar manipulator sketches, all joint rotation axes are perpendicular to the page. By convention, with 𝑥 horizontal (right) and 𝑦 vertical (up), the right-hand rule puts all 𝑧-axes pointing directly out of the page (⊙).

Geometric Meaning of the "Common Normal"

Given two arbitrary lines in space, 𝑧𝑖−1 and 𝑧𝑖:

  • Skew axes (general 3D case): There exists a unique line perpendicular to both axes. The line segment connecting them along this perpendicular line is called the common normal.
    • Its length is the link length 𝑎𝑖, and its direction defines the 𝑥𝑖-axis.
  • Intersecting axes: The distance between them is zero (𝑎𝑖 =0). The common normal is perpendicular to the plane containing both axes: 𝑥𝑖 = ±(𝑧𝑖−1 ×𝑧𝑖).
  • Parallel axes: Infinite common normals exist. We choose the one passing through the origin of the next frame, with length 𝑎𝑖 equal to the perpendicular distance between the parallel lines.

Part 2: The DH Transformation Matrix

Derivation from Four Elementary Transformations: To map coordinates from frame {𝑖} into frame {𝑖 −1}, we perform four consecutive basic transformations in a precise sequence:

  1. Rotate about 𝑧𝑖−1 by angle 𝜃𝑖 to align 𝑥𝑖−1 with 𝑥𝑖.
  2. Translate along 𝑧𝑖−1 by distance 𝑑𝑖 to bring 𝑥𝑖−1 and 𝑥𝑖 into the same plane.
  3. Translate along 𝑥𝑖 by distance 𝑎𝑖 to bring the origins together.
  4. Rotate about 𝑥𝑖 by angle 𝛼𝑖 to align 𝑧𝑖−1 with 𝑧𝑖.

Structural Breakdown of the Matrix

Every homogeneous transformation matrix has a four block structure: 𝑖−1𝑇𝑖=[𝑅3×3𝑃3×1𝟎1×31]

  1. Rotation Matrix (𝑅3×3): 𝑅=⎡⎢ ⎢⎣cos⁡𝜃𝑖−sin⁡𝜃𝑖cos⁡𝛼𝑖sin⁡𝜃𝑖sin⁡𝛼𝑖sin⁡𝜃𝑖cos⁡𝜃𝑖cos⁡𝛼𝑖−cos⁡𝜃𝑖sin⁡𝛼𝑖0sin⁡𝛼𝑖cos⁡𝛼𝑖⎤⎥ ⎥⎦=𝑅𝑧(𝜃𝑖)𝑅𝑥(𝛼𝑖)
    • The rotation is composed of rotation about 𝑧 followed by tilt about 𝑥.
  2. Translation Vector (𝑃3×1):𝑃=⎡⎢ ⎢⎣𝑎𝑖cos⁡𝜃𝑖𝑎𝑖sin⁡𝜃𝑖𝑑𝑖⎤⎥ ⎥⎦
    • The link length 𝑎𝑖 is along 𝑥𝑖. Expressed in frame {𝑖 −1}, its components are (𝑎𝑖cos⁡𝜃𝑖,𝑎𝑖sin⁡𝜃𝑖,0).
    • The offset 𝑑𝑖 is along 𝑧𝑖−1, contributing (0,0,𝑑𝑖).
    • Summing them gives the translation vector 𝑃.
  3. Perspective & Scale Row: [0 0 0 1], preserving the affine properties of rigid-body coordinate systems.

Homogeneous Vectors: Position vs. Direction

In homogeneous coordinates, vectors in 3D are extended to 4D: 𝐯=⎡⎢ ⎢ ⎢ ⎢ ⎢⎣𝑥𝑦𝑧𝑤⎤⎥ ⎥ ⎥ ⎥ ⎥⎦

  • Point / Position Vector (𝑤 =1): Subject to both rotation and translation. Multiplying 𝑖−1𝑇𝑖 by the origin [0,0,0,1]𝑇 extracts the 4th column, giving the origin of frame {𝑖} in frame {𝑖 −1} coordinates: 𝑖−1𝑇𝑖⎡⎢ ⎢ ⎢ ⎢ ⎢⎣0001⎤⎥ ⎥ ⎥ ⎥ ⎥⎦=⎡⎢ ⎢ ⎢ ⎢ ⎢⎣𝑎𝑖cos⁡𝜃𝑖𝑎𝑖sin⁡𝜃𝑖𝑑𝑖1⎤⎥ ⎥ ⎥ ⎥ ⎥⎦=[𝑃1]

Multiplying 𝑖−1𝑇𝑖 by the unit 𝑧-vector [0,0,1,0]𝑇 extracts the 3rd column, giving the orientation of 𝑧𝑖 in frame {𝑖 −1} coordinates: 𝑖−1𝑇𝑖⎡⎢ ⎢ ⎢ ⎢ ⎢⎣0010⎤⎥ ⎥ ⎥ ⎥ ⎥⎦=⎡⎢ ⎢ ⎢ ⎢ ⎢⎣sin⁡𝜃𝑖sin⁡𝛼𝑖−cos⁡𝜃𝑖sin⁡𝛼𝑖cos⁡𝛼𝑖0⎤⎥ ⎥ ⎥ ⎥ ⎥⎦=[𝑧𝑖0]

Part 4: DH Procedure for General Kinematic Chains

The Step-by-Step Kinematics Recipe: For any serial robot arm with 𝑛 joints:

  1. Assign coordinate frames {0} through {𝑛} using the rules from the start of the note.
  2. Build the DH Table: For each link 𝑖 =1,…,𝑛, extract 𝜃𝑖,𝛼𝑖,𝑎𝑖,𝑑𝑖.

Part 6: The Geometric Jacobian (General 6-DOF Formulation)

Differentiating position equations symbolically becomes impractical for 6-DOF spatial arms. The Geometric Jacobian uses rigid body mechanics to compute the Jacobian directly from frame geometry.

Matrix Anatomy (6 ×𝑛): In 3D space, an end-effector possesses 6 degrees of freedom: 3 linear velocities and 3 angular velocities. ˙𝑋=[𝑣𝑒𝜔𝑒]=⎡⎢ ⎢ ⎢ ⎢ ⎢ ⎢ ⎢ ⎢ ⎢ ⎢⎣˙𝑥˙𝑦˙𝑧𝜔𝑥𝜔𝑦𝜔𝑧⎤⎥ ⎥ ⎥ ⎥ ⎥ ⎥ ⎥ ⎥ ⎥ ⎥⎦=𝐽˙𝑞The 6 ×𝑛 Jacobian is partitioned into linear (𝐽𝑣) and angular (𝐽𝜔) sub-blocks: 𝐽=[𝐽𝑣𝐽𝜔]=[𝐽𝑣1𝐽𝑣2⋯𝐽𝑣𝑛𝐽𝜔1𝐽𝜔2⋯𝐽𝜔𝑛]
And Why Jacobian having 6 rows is because ˙𝑋 having 6 rows 3 for Linear Velocity Components and other 3 for Angular Velocity Components.

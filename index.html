Main Idea: SIFT extracts highly distinctive local invariant features that remain robust across changes in scale, in-plane rotation, 3D viewpoint, illumination, and affine distortion. Topic 13 Image Restoration: Inverse & Wiener Filters

Main Idea: When an image suffers from both optical blur (𝐻(𝑢,𝑣) ≠1) and additive noise, we operate in the frequency domain to deconvolve the blur while suppressing noise amplification.

1. Direct Inverse Filtering

The simplest deconvolution approach divides the observed degraded spectrum by the degradation transfer function:
ˆ𝐹(𝑢,𝑣)=𝐺(𝑢,𝑣)𝐻(𝑢,𝑣)=𝐻(𝑢,𝑣)𝐹(𝑢,𝑣)+𝑁(𝑢,𝑣)𝐻(𝑢,𝑣)=𝐹(𝑢,𝑣)+𝑁(𝑢,𝑣)𝐻(𝑢,𝑣)

The Noise Catastrophe of the Inverse Filter

The optical transfer function 𝐻(𝑢,𝑣) typical of camera shake, atmospheric turbulence, or out-of-focus blur acts as a low-pass filter, meaning its magnitude decays rapidly toward zero (𝐻(𝑢,𝑣) →0) at high frequencies.

Consequently, the noise term 𝑁(𝑢,𝑣)𝐻(𝑢,𝑣) blows up toward infinity at high frequencies! Even imperceptibly tiny noise completely swamps the reconstructed image signal:
lim|𝐻(𝑢,𝑣)|→0𝑁(𝑢,𝑣)𝐻(𝑢,𝑣)=∞

  • When Noise is Zero (𝐾 =0):
    ˆ𝐹(𝑢,𝑣)=𝐻∗(𝑢,𝑣)|𝐻(𝑢,𝑣)|2𝐺(𝑢,𝑣)=1𝐻(𝑢,𝑣)𝐺(𝑢,𝑣)(Reduces to the ideal Inverse Filter)
